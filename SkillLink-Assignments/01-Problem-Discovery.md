# Assignment 01 — Problem Discovery & Problem Statement

**Product Name:** SkillLink
**Product Type:** Worldwide Home & Professional Services Marketplace

---

## 1. Problem Title

**Finding Reliable, Available, and Suitable Service Professionals Is Difficult for Customers, While Skilled Workers Struggle to Reach Customers Who Need Them**

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Every day, people around the world need services such as plumbing, electrical repair, home cleaning, appliance repair, vehicle maintenance, personal care, or professional support like tutoring, accounting, or design work. Finding the right person for the job is often harder than it should be. Customers frequently rely on word-of-mouth recommendations from friends or family, scroll through social media groups, search inconsistently on Google, or call multiple numbers hoping someone is available and trustworthy. There is rarely a simple, unified way to compare workers, check their skills, see their availability, understand pricing, or judge their reliability before hiring them.

At the same time, skilled workers and independent service professionals often have the ability and willingness to work but lack a consistent channel to reach new customers. Many depend entirely on local reputation, personal networks, or occasional referrals, which limits how much work they can find, especially when starting out or moving to a new area.

This mismatch between people who need services and professionals who can provide them creates frustration, wasted time, and lost opportunity on both sides of the market. SkillLink is being explored as a potential digital solution to this real and widespread problem.

---

## 3. Target Users

### Who Are They?

SkillLink's problem space involves two primary groups:

**Customers**
Individuals or households who need help with home repairs, cleaning, technical support, vehicle care, personal services, or professional consulting. This includes homeowners, renters, small business owners, and anyone who occasionally or regularly needs a skilled person for a task they cannot or prefer not to do themselves.

**Service Professionals / Workers**
Skilled tradespeople, technicians, freelancers, and independent service providers who offer services such as plumbing, electrical work, carpentry, cleaning, vehicle repair, personal care, delivery, tutoring, or professional consulting, and who want a reliable way to find paying customers.

A third stakeholder, the platform operator, is relevant to the broader business but is not the focus of this assignment, which centers on the customer and service-provider experience.

### Who Is Affected?

This problem affects a very broad range of people, since almost everyone needs some kind of service at some point, whether it is an emergency repair, routine maintenance, or a one-time professional task. Service professionals across many trades and skill levels are similarly affected, particularly independent workers and small operators who do not have a large existing customer base or marketing budget.

### How Frequently Can the Problem Occur?

The problem can occur very frequently and in different forms:

* **Occasionally but urgently** — such as a broken water pipe, a power outage, or a car breakdown, where a customer needs help quickly and cannot afford a long search process.
* **Regularly** — such as recurring home cleaning, gardening, tutoring, or vehicle maintenance, where customers repeatedly go through the same search-and-compare process.
* **Continuously, from the worker's side** — since service professionals are generally always looking for their next customer, making customer discovery an ongoing, not one-time, challenge.

Because the need for services is constant and widespread, this is not a rare or edge-case problem; it is a common, recurring experience for both customers and professionals in cities and communities worldwide.

---

## 4. Current Situation

At present, most people rely on a mix of informal and fragmented methods to find service professionals:

* Asking friends, family, or neighbors for recommendations.
* Searching general platforms like Google or Facebook and hoping the results are relevant, current, and trustworthy.
* Scrolling through local community groups or classified listings.
* Calling multiple contacts or businesses one by one to check availability and price.
* Relying on a worker they have used before, even if that person is busy, unavailable, or not the best fit for a specific task.

On the professional side, many workers depend on personal reputation, local visibility (such as a shop sign or parked vehicle), or referrals from past customers. Workers with fewer existing connections, or those who are new to an area, often have a much harder time getting consistent work, even if their skills are strong.

There is generally no single, organized, worldwide system where customers and service professionals can easily and reliably connect across this wide range of service categories.

---

## 5. Existing Solutions

Several types of solutions currently exist, each addressing part of the problem:

* **General search engines and social media** — useful for discovery but not designed specifically for comparing service providers, checking availability, or verifying trust.
* **Local classifieds and community groups** — allow workers to post services or customers to ask for recommendations, but listings are often outdated, unorganized, or hard to search.
* **Word-of-mouth and personal networks** — still one of the most trusted methods, but limited to the people someone already knows and cannot scale.
* **Regional or niche service apps** — some cities or countries have individual apps for specific service types (for example, a cleaning-only app or a car-repair-only app), but these are usually limited to one category, one region, or one type of service rather than covering the full range of everyday needs in one place.
* **Traditional service businesses with their own staff** — reliable in some cases, but often more expensive, less flexible, and not available for every service type or location.

---

## 6. Problems With Existing Solutions

* **Fragmented across categories** — a customer may need to use several different apps or methods depending on the type of service, instead of one consistent place.
* **Limited to specific regions** — many existing services are only available in certain cities or countries, leaving many customers and workers worldwide without a comparable option.
* **Lack of trust indicators** — general search or social media results rarely show verified information about a worker's skill, reliability, or past performance.
* **Inconsistent availability information** — customers often do not know whether a worker is currently free, busy, or accepting new jobs until after contacting them directly.
* **Time-consuming comparison process** — comparing multiple workers on price, skill, and availability usually requires several separate phone calls or messages.
* **Limited reach for workers** — many skilled professionals, especially independent or small-scale workers, have no easy way to advertise their availability to a wider pool of potential customers beyond their existing network.
* **No unified experience across service types** — a platform that works well for finding a plumber may not translate to finding a tutor, a photographer, or an accountant, since these existing tools were not built to scale across dozens of different service categories.

---

## 7. Impact of the Problem

The problem has real consequences for both sides of the market:

**For customers:**

* Wasted time searching, calling, and waiting for responses.
* Increased stress during urgent situations, such as home emergencies.
* Risk of hiring unreliable, unverified, or unsuitable workers.
* Difficulty comparing prices and options fairly.

**For service professionals:**

* Missed income opportunities due to limited visibility.
* Inconsistent or unpredictable work, especially for independent workers.
* Difficulty growing a customer base beyond personal referrals.
* Unequal opportunity compared to workers who happen to have stronger existing networks.

**For communities and markets more broadly:**

* Skilled labor remains underutilized in areas where demand for services exists but connection is difficult.
* Customers may settle for lower-quality service simply because it was the only option they could find quickly.

---

## 8. Why This Problem Is Worth Solving

This problem is worth solving because it affects nearly everyone, repeatedly, across almost every country and city. Services are a basic and ongoing need, not a one-time purchase, which means the difficulty of finding the right professional is experienced again and again by the same customers over time.

At the same time, the problem represents a missed opportunity for a very large group of skilled workers who could be earning more consistent income if they had a simpler, wider-reaching way to connect with people who need their services.

Solving this problem well could meaningfully improve everyday life for customers and create fairer, broader income opportunities for service professionals, regardless of their location. Because the problem is general and not tied to one city or one service category, a worldwide, multi-category solution like SkillLink has the potential to address a genuinely large and recurring need.

## 9. Evidence / Problem Validation

The observations in this document are based on common, widely recognized user experiences with finding and hiring service professionals, rather than on formal survey data, interviews, or statistical research. No specific percentages, survey results, or research figures are claimed, as none have been collected at this stage.

This problem should be treated as an assumption to be validated in later stages of the project, for example through:

* Informal conversations or interviews with potential customers about how they currently find service professionals.
* Informal conversations with workers or tradespeople about how they currently find customers.
* Observation of existing platforms, community groups, and classifieds to understand common complaints or gaps.

Future assignments or project phases may include structured validation methods such as user interviews or surveys to confirm and refine the assumptions made here.

---

## 10. Key Problem Insights

* The core difficulty is not a lack of skilled workers, but a lack of an easy, reliable way to connect them with the customers who need their services.
* The problem is two-sided: customers struggle to find workers, and workers struggle to find customers, at the same time.
* The problem repeats itself regularly, since most people need some kind of service more than once.
* Existing solutions are fragmented by category and region, rather than offering one consistent, worldwide experience.
* Trust, availability, and ease of comparison are the most commonly missing pieces in the current process.
* Because the problem spans many service categories (home repair, cleaning, technical support, vehicle care, personal care, and professional services), a single unified marketplace has a meaningful opportunity to simplify the experience for both sides.

---

## 11. Problem Summary

Customers around the world regularly struggle to find service professionals who are reliable, available, and suitable for their needs, often relying on scattered, informal, and time-consuming methods. At the same time, skilled workers and independent service professionals often lack an effective way to reach new customers beyond their personal networks. Existing solutions only partially address this problem, are often limited to specific regions or service categories, and lack consistent trust and availability information. This creates a recurring, widespread problem affecting both customers and professionals, and represents a clear opportunity for a unified, worldwide services marketplace.
